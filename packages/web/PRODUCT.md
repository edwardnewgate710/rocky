# Product

## Platform

web

## Users

Primary: competitive and rated players — people who play chess seriously and care about fairness, precision, and reliability, not just casual fun. They arrive expecting the game to feel tight: every move validated, every interaction responsive, nothing flaky under time pressure. The board is their workspace; everything else in the UI is in service of the game in progress or the improvement they're chasing between games.

## Product Purpose

Gambit exists to be the place competitive players trust for their games: fast, reliable, fair, and beautiful, with AI coaching built in as a first-class layer rather than a bolt-on. Success means players keep coming back daily because the platform outperforms Chess.com and Lichess on usability, performance, and overall experience — not just parity, but a step ahead.

## Positioning

The world's most advanced online chess platform, combining competitive play, AI coaching, and premium user experience.

## Brand Personality

Precise, premium, timeless. The reference point is Lichess's clarity and no-nonsense, board-first minimalism — but taken further: more polished typography, more considered visual craft, without adding clutter. Confidence should come from restraint, not decoration.

## Anti-references

Two things to explicitly avoid: the generic SaaS-dashboard look (cards everywhere, gradient heroes, tool-for-work aesthetics — this is a game, not a B2B app), and the cluttered/gamified chess-site look (busy badges, streak counters, and noisy gamification competing with the board for attention).

## Design Principles

- **The board is the product.** Every screen should feel like it exists in service of the game in progress or the player's improvement — nothing decorative competes with the board for attention.
- **Precision over decoration.** Visual choices should read as considered craft (tight spacing, deliberate typography, restrained color), not gamified noise or trend-chasing flourish.
- **Trust through polish.** Competitive players judge fairness and seriousness by how tight the UI feels — janky transitions or inconsistent states erode trust as much as a real bug would.
- **Timeless over trendy.** Favor choices that will still look premium in five years over whatever reads as "modern" this quarter.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.1 AA baseline (matches the existing Lighthouse a11y ≥ 0.95 CI gate), plus colorblind-safe board and piece cues: move highlights, legal-move indicators, and premove/last-move markers must remain distinguishable without relying on hue alone (shape/pattern/contrast redundancy, not color-only signaling).
